About

I am a Member of Clinical Staff at Abridge, working at the intersection of artificial intelligence, clinical research, and product development. I design evaluations of AI-generated clinical outputs, study how AI affects documentation and clinical workflows, and translate clinical needs and research findings into priorities for model and product development.

My technical background includes a PhD in Biomedical Informatics from Stanford University and an MA in Biostatistics from the University of California, Berkeley. My work draws on machine learning, large language models, causal inference, clinical informatics, and health outcomes research. Before joining Abridge, I was a Research Scientist at the Palo Alto Veterans Institute for Research and an affiliated researcher in Stanford Cardiovascular Medicine. I collaborated with multidisciplinary teams across the VA, Stanford, and a major pharmaceutical company to study treatment gaps and advance cardiovascular disease prevention.

I also bring more than 16 years of experience as a registered nurse across medical-surgical step-down, acute care, oncology, trauma, and centralized telemetry. This clinical background helps me recognize problems that may not be visible in the data alone and evaluate whether an AI system is genuinely useful within patient-care workflows.

I am interested in opportunities where I can set clinical AI research and evaluation strategy, identify high-impact problems, build interdisciplinary partnerships, and guide the development of technology that is safe, useful, and grounded in the realities of patient care.

Areas of Focus

My work combines technical research, clinical expertise, and strategic thinking. I focus not only on whether an AI system performs well, but also on whether it addresses the right clinical problem and can be integrated responsibly into real-world care.

  • Clinical AI Research and Evaluation: Design rigorous evaluations of accuracy, safety, clinical usefulness, generalizability, and workflow impact.
  • Machine Learning and Language Models: Develop and evaluate computational approaches using clinical notes, structured EHR data, administrative data, and patient-reported outcomes.
  • Research and Product Strategy: Translate clinical problems and research findings into model priorities, product decisions, and development direction.
  • Biostatistics and Causal Inference: Apply quantitative methods to measure clinical outcomes and assess healthcare interventions.
  • Cross-Functional Leadership: Build alignment among clinicians, researchers, data scientists, engineers, product leaders, health systems, and industry partners.
